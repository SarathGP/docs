## Overview

The **EU Withdrawal Button** adds a simple, legally compliant way for your customers to cancel
("withdraw from") their orders within the cooling-off period required by EU law.

It places a small floating button on your storefront. When a shopper clicks it, the app walks them
through a short form that records their cancellation request, sends them a confirmation email, and
stores a tidy record for you inside the Shopify admin.

You don't need to know anything about Shopify apps or EU law to use it — this page explains both in
plain language.

## What is the "right of withdrawal"?

In the European Union, online shoppers have a legal **right of withdrawal**. This comes from an EU
law called the *Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU)*.

In simple terms:

<Card title="The 14-day rule" icon="calendar-days" horizontal>
  A customer who buys online can change their mind and cancel the order within **14 days** of
  receiving it — **without giving any reason**. This is often called the *cooling-off period*.
</Card>

To comply with this law, stores must give customers a **clear and easy way** to declare that they
are withdrawing. The EU even publishes a standard "model withdrawal form" that the wording should be
based on. The EU Withdrawal Button does this for you automatically.

<Note>
This app helps you offer a compliant withdrawal process. It is not legal advice. If you are unsure
about your obligations, check with a legal professional for your specific situation.
</Note>

## Why use this app?

Without a dedicated tool, customers usually email or call to cancel, and you track everything by
hand. That's slow, easy to get wrong, and hard to prove if a regulator ever asks. This app:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Gives customers a clear path" icon="hand-pointer">
    A visible button and a guided, step-by-step form — no confusion about how to cancel.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Sends legal proof automatically" icon="envelope-circle-check">
    Every confirmed withdrawal triggers a confirmation email — the durable record the law expects.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Keeps a full audit trail" icon="list-check">
    Every request is logged with a reference number, timeline of events, and the exact items.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Handles the rules for you" icon="shield-check">
    It automatically checks the 14-day window and skips orders that are exempt.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## How it works at a glance

<Steps>
  <Step title="A customer clicks the button">
    The floating **"Withdraw from contract"** button appears on your storefront. The customer looks
    up their order with their order number and email.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They choose what to cancel and confirm">
    They pick which items (and how many) they want to withdraw, optionally add a reason, and confirm
    using the legally required wording.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Everyone gets a record">
    The customer receives a confirmation email and an on-screen receipt with a reference number. You
    get a notification and a new entry in your admin.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You process the withdrawal">
    From the admin you review the request, mark it **In progress**, **Completed**, or **Rejected**,
    and issue any refund through Shopify as usual.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Key terms

A few words you'll see throughout this documentation and inside the app:

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Right of withdrawal** | The EU customer's legal right to cancel an online order within the cooling-off period. |
| **Withdrawal window** | The time period a customer has to cancel. Defaults to **14 days** from delivery. |
| **Declaration** | The customer's statement that they want to withdraw from the contract. |
| **Reference number** | A unique ID for each request, like `WD-2026-0001`. |
| **Compliance status** | The app's automatic tracking of the request (e.g. *Confirmed*, *Confirmation sent*). |
| **Processing status** | Your own tracking of where you are with the request (*In progress*, *Completed*, *Rejected*). |
| **Exempt order** | An order the law doesn't require withdrawal for (e.g. B2B, subscriptions, some digital goods). |
