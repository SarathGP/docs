## Overview

The **Withdrawal window** section controls how long a customer has to cancel their order. The defaults
follow EU law, so most stores never need to change these.

## Options

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Withdrawal period (days)" icon="hourglass-half">
    The length of the cooling-off period — how many days after delivery a customer can still withdraw.
    The default is **14 days**, which is the EU legal minimum. You can set any value between **7 and
    60 days**.

    <Warning>
    EU law requires a minimum of 14 days. Lowering this below 14 may put your store out of compliance.
    Increasing it (offering a longer window) is always allowed.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Estimated delivery (days)" icon="truck">
    A fallback used only when Shopify doesn't have an exact delivery date for an order. The app adds
    this number of days to the ship date to estimate when delivery happened, then counts the
    withdrawal period from there. The default is **5 days**, and you can set **1 to 60 days**.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## How the window is calculated

The cooling-off period is always measured from **delivery**, not from the order date:

<Steps>
  <Step title="If Shopify knows the delivery date">
    The withdrawal period is counted from that exact delivery date.
  </Step>
  <Step title="If the delivery date is unknown">
    The app estimates delivery as the **ship date + your estimated delivery days**, then counts the
    withdrawal period from that estimate.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If a customer tries to withdraw after the window has closed, they're told the period has ended and
can't complete the request.

<Tip>
Set the estimated delivery to roughly match how long shipping usually takes for your store. A
realistic estimate keeps the calculated window fair for orders without a delivery date.
</Tip>
